## Summary

The Critical parser bypass of remediation spec §6 is closed. A contract section now has an ADDRESS, not a name prefix: `spec_linter/sections.py` is the single authority for exact-slug addressing AND for what counts as live Markdown structure, and `build_report.py` routes all six fixed-name sections through it. Four adversarial review rounds turned a narrow fix into a structural one — the decisive insight, and the sentence worth carrying into the rest of the program, is that **the set of constructions trusted to delimit evidence must equal the set monitored for abuse of that trust**. Closed: prefix decoys, stray and demoted headings, fenced/commented pseudo-headings, fence run-length confusion, a plain unrecognised heading, and the 15 vocabulary headings nothing used to watch. Plus a table-anchored safety net so scope games cannot hide a blocking row, severity read as words (`Critical (F1)` was evading on our own reports), and v3.19.0's authorized fix-round override so an overrun is attributed rather than falsified. PR A of the remediation program; unblocks PRs B–I.

## Lessons Learned

### Process

- Four rounds, four Criticals, each one layer below the last: prefix → heading level → opaque region → unwatched boundary heading → cell grammar. Adversarial review earned its cost here more than anywhere in the program; a single round would have shipped a bypass *easier to trigger* than the original bug.
- Reproduce before fixing, every time. Each round I re-ran the reviewer's scenario against the live contract first — twice that changed what the right fix was, and once (the plain `## Notes` vector) it revealed the reported instance was the narrow case, not the class.
- Budget exhaustion is a decision point, not a formality. At 2/2 with a live Critical open, the honest move was to surface it and let the maintainer raise the budget — and then to make that overrun expressible in the contract instead of silently exceeding or falsifying it.

### Technical

- A gate that cries wolf gets routed around, so precision is a security property. The blunt whole-document scan would have blocked a legitimate `## Autonomous Decisions` row reading "Important"; anchoring on table structure kept fail-closed *and* quiet on valid reports.
- One primitive, not two. The row scanner initially re-implemented opacity unaware of fences; extracting `content_lines()` removed the drift instead of patching the second copy.
- Fixing the matcher exposed a real defect in a shipped artifact: with decoration no longer hiding meaning, an already-merged report failed on an `Important (F5)` row whose resolution said "fixed across rounds 1–2" instead of the contract's `fixed in <ref>`. Corrected the cell, not the rule.
